A **proposal** is the customer-facing version of an estimate: a clean, branded document the customer can read, review, and sign online. There's no separate step to create it. Every estimate opens on its proposal page, called the **Proposal Preview**, and that page is where you build the estimate and get it ready to send.

For an estimate built from your price book, the **Proposal Preview** is also the estimate editor. You add work areas and items right on the page, and the prices update as you go. See [Build an estimate](/guides/estimates/create) for the step-by-step.

<Note>
An estimate created with the **From Scratch** option also opens on its proposal, where you add the line items yourself. See [Create an estimate from scratch](/guides/estimates/create-from-scratch).
</Note>

<Tip>
You don't have to mark the estimate **Sold** to send a proposal. When the customer signs it, the estimate is marked **Sold** automatically.
</Tip>

## Open the proposal

You land on the **Proposal Preview** as soon as you create a new estimate. To come back to it later:

- On the **Estimates** page, click the estimate's name, or open the row's **⋮** menu and choose **Edit Estimate** (while it's **In Progress**) or **View Estimate** (any other status).
- On a project's **Estimates** tab, open the row's **⋮** menu and choose **View Proposal** or **Preview Proposal**.

The link at the top of the page takes you back where you came from: **Back to project** or **Back to estimates**.

## What's on the Proposal Preview

The page shows the proposal the way the customer will see it, with your editing tools and a few team-only details mixed in. From top to bottom you'll find:

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Header and branding">
    Your company logo and details, plus a **Sent Date** once the proposal has been sent. Next to the page title, the status badge shows the estimate's status, and you can change it from there if your role allows it. The header also holds **Save as Draft**, **Send Proposal**, and the **⋮** menu (see [More actions](#more-actions)).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="To / From">
    The **To** block shows the customer and the job address. The **From** block shows the salesperson whose name and contact details appear on the proposal. You can change who that is from a dropdown here.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Notes to Client">
    A personal message to the customer. It starts empty. Add one to make the proposal feel tailored. (See **Edit the note and price display**.)
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="About, reviews, and media">
    Your company's "about" section, customer reviews for your branch, and any photos or videos you attach to the proposal or to individual line items.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Project Allowances">
    An optional section for custom terms, allowances, or notes about the work. It starts empty. Add one when you want to spell out something specific for the customer. (See **Edit the note and price display**.)
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Service Breakdown">
    The itemized list of work, grouped by work area. On a price-book estimate, this is where you add and change work areas, items, and **Days of work**. Use the **Client View Settings** switches (**Qty**, **Unit Price**, **Total Price**) to choose which numbers the customer sees.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Price Summary">
    Subtotal, discount, taxes, total, required deposit, an optional **Payment Schedule** (when it's turned on for your branch), and the payment options the customer can choose from (including financing, when offered). On a price-book estimate, some roles also see a team-only **Job Breakdown** card here.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## What only your team sees

On a price-book estimate, a banner at the top of the Service Breakdown reminds you: **Only your team sees the marked details**. Factors, additional costs, notes about automatically added items, and the **Job Breakdown** are hidden from the customer. They see the items, the quantities, and the prices you switch on in **Client View Settings**.

Team-only details are marked with a crossed-out eye icon. Hover it to see **Only your team sees this**.

The **Job Breakdown** card in the Price Summary shows where the money goes: job cost, margin, labor hours, and travel. Click **See full breakdown →** for the details. What you see depends on your role:

- **Admin** and **Ops Manager** see the full card, including costs and margin.
- **Crew Leader** sees the labor and travel figures only, with no money amounts.
- Other roles don't see the card.

## Set who the proposal is from

In the **From** block, use the salesperson dropdown to choose whose name, phone, and contact details appear on the proposal. It defaults to the person who created the estimate, which is usually exactly right. Change it only if someone else should be the customer's point of contact.

## Attach photos and videos

To make the proposal more convincing, you can add media:

- **Proposal-level media**: photos or videos for the job as a whole.
- **Line-item media**: attach photos or videos to a specific service so the customer can see exactly what that work involves. Look for **Attach photos or videos for this line item** under each item, then **Upload Media**.

<Tip>
Media you add here only shows on the proposal. It doesn't change the estimate's pricing.
</Tip>

When payment schedules are turned on for your branch, the **Price Summary** includes a **Payment Schedule** section, just below **Required Deposit**. A payment schedule breaks the total into named payments (for example a deposit, a progress payment, and a final payment) so the customer can see exactly what they'll pay and when.

<Note>
If you don't see a Payment Schedule section, it isn't turned on for your branch yet. Check with your manager.
</Note>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the editor">
    In the Payment Schedule section, click **Add Payment Schedule** (or the **pencil** icon if a schedule already exists). The **Payment schedule** editor opens, showing the proposal's total price at the top. If your branch requires a deposit, the schedule may already include a **Project Deposit** payment based on your branch's deposit settings.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add a payment">
    Click **Add Payment** and give it a name (for example, *Deposit* or *Final Payment*). Enter its amount, then use the **$ / %** toggle to choose whether that amount is a fixed dollar figure or a percentage of the total. A percentage can't be more than 100.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Attach line items (optional)">
    Use **+ Add Items** on a payment to tie specific services from the Service Breakdown to it, so the customer can see what each payment covers.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Balance the schedule">
    As you edit, a running total shows how much of the proposal is scheduled, with a progress bar. When the payments match the total, you'll see a **Fully Scheduled** badge. If they add up to more than the total, you'll see how much you're **over** and won't be able to save until you bring it back down.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save">
    Click **Save schedule**. The schedule appears in the Price Summary and travels with the proposal to the customer. If you remove every payment, the button becomes **Remove schedule**. Click **Cancel** to discard your changes.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
If your branch sets a **Max Deposit Amount**, the **Project Deposit** can't be scheduled for more than that cap. When the deposit would go over it, the editor lowers it to the cap and shows the capped figure with **capped at the branch's max deposit** beneath that payment. Only the capped amount counts toward the running total, so the schedule can fall short of the proposal total. Add or adjust the other payments to cover the rest. Renaming the deposit or changing its amount turns it into an ordinary payment, and the cap no longer applies.
</Note>

<Note>
The **Project Deposit** keeps itself in sync: if you change the proposal's pricing after building a schedule, that payment automatically updates to match the latest **Required Deposit**, so you don't have to touch it. A schedule that's only the Project Deposit stays current on its own, with no reminder.

For any other payments you added, changing the proposal's pricing or line items shows a reminder on the Payment Schedule section to review and update them so they stay in sync with the latest total.

The Project Deposit stops updating itself once the deposit has been invoiced, or if you've edited its name or amount so it's no longer the automatic deposit. From then on it keeps the amount it was set to.
</Note>

<Tip>
When it's time to bill a payment, create its invoice from the project's **Invoices** tab. See [Invoice a scheduled payment](/guides/billing/create-an-invoice#invoice-a-scheduled-payment).
</Tip>

## More actions

The **⋮** menu in the page header holds a few more options:

- **Present to Client**: walk the customer through the proposal and collect their signature in person. (On a phone, this is its own button.)
- **Download PDF**: open the proposal as a print-ready PDF. Available once the proposal has been saved.
- **Update to current pricing**: Admins, Ops Managers, and Sales Admins only, on a price-book estimate you can still edit. See [Refresh the pricing](#refresh-the-pricing).
- **Inspection Report**: open the estimate's inspection report, marked **Done** or **Not started**. Shown when inspection reports are turned on for your workspace.

<Note>
If inspection reports are turned on for your workspace, a price-book estimate needs a completed inspection report before you can send or present its proposal. Until then, **Send Proposal** and **Present to Client** are disabled and a banner reads **Fill in the inspection report before sending**. Click **Fill in inspection report →** in the banner to open it.
</Note>

## Refresh the pricing

Each estimate keeps the prices and settings from when it was created, so later changes to your price book don't quietly change a quote you're working on. To bring an estimate up to date, an Admin, Ops Manager, or Sales Admin can open the **⋮** menu and choose **Update to current pricing**, then confirm with **Update pricing**. The proposal then uses the latest prices, labels, tax rules, branch settings, and related items. Items already on the proposal that were removed from your catalog stay on it.

## Save your work

You don't need to save the estimate. Every change to work areas, items, days of work, and the discount saves automatically, and the prices update a moment later.

**Save as Draft** saves the rest of the page: your **Client View Settings** and the order of the line items. The first time you use it, it also creates the proposal itself. Until you've saved the proposal or sent it, the page is only a preview. When those settings have changed, you'll see **Unsaved changes** next to the button. A draft stays fully editable and the customer can't see it yet.
